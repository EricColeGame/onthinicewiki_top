## Core Narrative: Desperation, Morality, and Crime

The driving engine of *Walking on Thin Ice* is its emotional core: how far will a mother go when legitimate systems fail her family? Rather than portraying a glamorous criminal mastermind, the script grounds Kang Eun-soo as an exhausted, everyday woman navigating an unforgiving healthcare crisis.

### Synopsis and Thematic Pillars

When Kang Eun-soo's husband is diagnosed with terminal pancreatic cancer, mounting medical bills push the household toward financial ruin. In an accidental twist of fate, Eun-soo stumbles upon an abandoned cache of illicit substances valued at hundreds of billions of won. 

Faced with the imminent loss of her partner and the destitution of her young daughter, she makes the harrowing calculation to liquidate the contraband herself. What follows is a descent into a labyrinth of shadowy cartels, undercover officers, and double-dealing intermediaries.

Key narrative themes explored throughout the 12 episodes include:
- **Parental Desperation vs. Ethical Boundaries:** The gradual erosion of moral boundaries in pursuit of family survival.
- **The Dual Lives of Ordinary Citizens:** The contrast between everyday domestic duties and underground criminal negotiations.
- **Systemic Failures:** How gaps in socioeconomic safety nets push working-class individuals toward illicit alternatives.
- **The Psychology of the Chase:** The intricate cat-and-mouse dynamic between seasoned investigators and an amateur criminal acting out of pure survival instinct.
